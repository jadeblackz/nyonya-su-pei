A typical class

Three unhurried steps

Most classes run from morning until early afternoon. Seats are limited.

  1. 1

    Meet at the market

    We begin at Chowrasta Market. Su Pei shows you the herbs, fish, and spices you will cook with — and why they matter.

  2. 2

    Cook together, slowly

    In a clean, small kitchen you grind rempah, wrap, stew, and fry. Everything is from scratch. Questions are welcome.

  3. 3

    Sit down to lunch

    You eat what you made. Printed English recipes come home with you, so the dishes can live in your kitchen too.

On the table

Dishes you may cook

Menus change with the market and the season. These are family recipes Su Pei returns to often.

Penang Assam Laksa

Tamarind broth, mackerel, torch ginger, and hay koh.

Ayam Buah Keluak

Chicken stewed with rare black keluak nuts.

Popiah Basah

Fresh spring rolls with turnip, prawn, and house chilli.

Roti Jala

Lattice crepes served with fragrant Nyonya curry.

Nyonya Kuih

Ondeh-ondeh, kueh lapis, and festive sweets.

Rempah from scratch

The spice paste that holds every dish together.

The Nyonya

Award-winning chef, patient teacher

Nyonya Su Pei holds a Professional Diploma in Malaysian Heritage Culinary and has taught for more than ten years — in Penang, in Singapore, and at private tables. She shares family recipes, the stories behind them, and the practical skill to cook them again at home.
